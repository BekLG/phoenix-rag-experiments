### **Summary of "The Abilene Paradox: The Management of Agreement" by Jerry B. Harvey**

**Main Subject & Purpose:**
This seminal article introduces the **Abilene Paradox**, a phenomenon where organizations (or groups) take actions **contrary to their true desires**, leading to counterproductive outcomes. Harvey argues that **inability to manage agreement**—not conflict—is the core dysfunction in modern organizations. The article blends a **narrative anecdote** (a family trip to Abilene no one wanted) with **organizational case studies** (a failing R&D project and Watergate) to illustrate the paradox’s mechanics, causes, and remedies.

**Key Sections & Topics:**
1. **The Abilene Paradox Defined**
   - Organizations act against their own interests due to **mismanaged agreement** (e.g., collective decisions diverging from individual preferences).
   - **Corollary**: Dysfunction stems from **fear of disagreement**, not conflict itself.

2. **Symptoms of the Paradox**
   Six subsymptoms reveal the paradox in action:
   - Private agreement on problems/solutions.
   - Failure to communicate true desires.
   - Collective decisions contradicting shared preferences.
   - Post-decision frustration and blame-shifting.

3. **Case Studies**
   - **Ozyx Corporation**: Executives privately knew a doomed R&D project was unsustainable but avoided confrontation, leading to financial ruin.
   - **Watergate**: Nixon’s aides privately opposed illegal surveillance but colluded to avoid "disloyalty," escalating the scandal.

4. **Psychological Underpinnings**
   Five interlinked dynamics drive the paradox:
   - **Action Anxiety**: Fear of consequences for voicing dissent.
   - **Negative Fantasies**: Imagined worse outcomes from confrontation.
   - **Real Risk**: Existential threats (e.g., job loss, ostracism).
   - **Fear of Separation**: Dread of alienation from the group.
   - **Psychological Reversal**: Prioritizing imagined risks over real ones, ensuring failure.

5. **Solutions & Redefinitions**
   - **Bypass Abilene**: Reframe concepts like *victim/victimizer* (all are victims), *collusion* (shared responsibility), and *confrontation* (facing agreed-upon truths).
   - **Leadership**: Any member can break the cycle by **speaking truth to collective agreement**.
   - **Diagnostic Survey**: A tool to distinguish **phony conflict** (agreement in disguise) from **real conflict**.

**Factual Claims & Terminology:**
- **Core Claim**: Organizations fail not from conflict but from **suppressed agreement**.
- **Key Terms**: *Action Anxiety*, *Groupthink*, *Phony Conflict*, *Separation Anxiety*, *Psychological Reversal*.
- **Examples**: Dominoes game analogy, Ozyx’s "turkey" project, Watergate’s illegal surveillance.

**Structure & Style:**
- **Narrative Hook**: Personal anecdote grounds the abstract concept.
- **Theoretical Framework**: Blends psychology (e.g., Hamlet’s soliloquy) with organizational behavior.
- **Practical Focus**: Ends with actionable diagnostics and redefinitions.

**Significance:**
Harvey’s work critiques **conformity pressures** and **hierarchical blame**, advocating for **radical transparency** to align actions with true intentions. The paradox remains a cornerstone of **organizational behavior**, explaining failures from corporate scandals to policy disasters.
